What are we trying to predict?

We use past sales to estimate future sales for each store and product category.

Why it matters

Good forecasts help plan stock, staffing and purchases. Too much stock ties up money; too little means missed sales.

What we forecast

Daily sales for each store and product category over the next {{ horizon_days }} days ({{ date_range_short }}).

Where the data comes from

  • Past sales (train.csv) — about 3 million records, 54 stores, 33 product categories, from 1 January 2013 to 15 August 2017
  • Future period to forecast (test.csv) — {{ horizon_days }} days, 28,512 combinations of store and product
  • No missing values — data is complete

The analysis was built and run in Google Colab. The notebook is kept as the full record. This website just shows the results — it does not rerun the model.

What i learned

  • Some high-volume product categories are harder to predict, especially Grocery I and Beverages.
  • A few stores consistently show larger prediction differences.
  • Certain dates are harder to predict than others, suggesting that calendar patterns do not explain every demand change.
  • The model performs better than the simple weekly-pattern benchmark on the test period.
  • All predictions are non-negative and complete — {{ "{:,}".format(table_total_rows) }} forecasts ready for use.

These points are based only on the existing results. They do not guarantee future inventory decisions — they highlight where to look more closely.

WAPE (Weighted Absolute Percentage Error): the technical metric used to measure forecast error. MAE/RMSE are additional error metrics reported by the notebook.

Modeling approach

  • Model: Ridge Regression (StandardScaler + Ridge α=1.0)
  • Baseline: Seasonal Naive (prediction = sales 7 days earlier, i.e., lag_7)
  • Features (14): store, product category (one-hot encoding), promotion items, year, month, day, day of week, week, quarter, weekend flag, lag_1, lag_7, lag_14, rolling_mean_7
  • Validation: chronological validation — chronological split at 2017-08-01 (train 2,974,158 rows, validation 26,730 rows)
  • Forecasting: recursive forecasting — recursive 16-day horizon where each day’s forecast feeds the next day’s lag features
  • Feature count: 14 | Validation cutoff: 2017-08-01 | Forecast horizon: 16 days (2017-08-16 to 2017-08-31)
